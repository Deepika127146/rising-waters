Why Random Split Overestimates Generalization

In an 80:20 stratified split, 85.9% of test feature combinations and 100% of monitoring stations were already present in the training set. Because climatological normals are static per basin, the model memorizes known catchment identities rather than discovering dynamic rainfall-runoff mechanics.

🌐

Why Spatial & Temporal Splits are More Demanding

When evaluated on completely unseen river basins (Station-Grouped CV), accuracy drops to 60.25% (ROC-AUC: 0.7842). In chronological testing, models trained on flood-heavy pre-2012 inventories over-predict floods on modern digital archives (where 80.6% of days are normal), dropping precision to 25.12%.

⚖️

The Base Rate Fallacy in Operational Floods

Natural floods occur on only 1.91% of days across all monitoring stations. Even with 86% specificity, the 14% false-positive rate on the 98.1% normal days vastly outnumbers true flood occurrences, lowering precision to 3.58% without live radar and rainfall telemetry.

🎯

Susceptibility Demonstrator vs. Operational Warning

This system is an educational catchment susceptibility demonstrator. It identifies baseline topographical and climatological vulnerability under seasonal rainfall regimes. Dynamic flood warning requires live 24–48h NWP rainfall forecasts, radar feeds, and river-gauge telemetry from official agencies.
